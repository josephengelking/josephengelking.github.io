Parallel Elastic Ankle Prosthetic Component

Validated a variable-stiffness torsional spring for a lighter ankle prosthesis by designing a dynamometer rig and a two-motor walking simulator.

Current lower-limb prosthetic devices are too heavy, which requires the user to expend excessive energy while performing various tasks. To lower the weight of the artificial limb, one approach is to reduce the size of the motor necessary to actuate the ankle, which can be done through putting an elastic component in parallel with the motor.

At Dr. Edgar Bolivar Nieto’s Wearable Robotics Lab (WeRoLab) at the University of Notre Dame, in collaboration with the Compliant Mechanisms and Robotics Group at Brigham Young University (BYU), I validated a variable stiffness torsional spring by designing a dynamometer testing rig. This involved controlling two motors simultaneously to simulate walking with a Raspberry Pi 5 and PID control.

I documented my contribution to this project in the experimental design section of a paper in progress to be submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters. Additionally, I presented a poster at Notre Dame’s Summer Undergraduate Research Symposium.
